# Social Production Operations Model

Draft status: working draft, not final policy.

This file is for human readers and planning review. It can change as governance, moderation, and distribution rules are tightened.

This document is the current working draft for how Social Production governs projects, organizes production, and distributes outputs.

It combines the older split between governance, distribution, and production drafts so the operating model can be reviewed in one place.

## Overview

Social Production exists to organize collective production and distribution outside capitalist market structures. Production on the platform is for use, not for exchange, not for profit, and not for sale. What is produced belongs to the community and is distributed according to need and agreed plans.

The platform itself is the organization. There are no separate membership tiers, no permanent authorities, and no governance bodies outside transparent on-chain processes. Funding a project gives no additional governance authority. Contributions go to the collective, not to influence.

## Core Principles

- Collective ownership of outputs and infrastructure
- Need-based distribution rather than exchange
- Transparent governance through on-chain records
- Revocable roles rather than permanent authority
- Production planning based on visible demand signals
- Trial, iteration, and revision through governance rather than fixed doctrine
- Optional badges or achievements, if shown, are cosmetic only and never change governance standing, access, or distribution rights

## Projects

A project can be created by any user on the platform. The creator becomes its first manager. Governance then happens through project membership.

Each project has an attached forum or chat so members can communicate continuously throughout the full project lifecycle. This is where people discuss ideas, refine plans, coordinate work, respond to problems, and review outcomes as the project develops.

### Project Types

- Production: collective manufacture, growing, building
- Service: skills, labor, care, maintenance, mutual aid, direct need support, land asset management, storage management, and other non-production work organized through full projects

Mutual aid requests such as emergency support or help moving fit within the service category and are treated as valid productive activity.

Service work can still be light in scope while remaining a project. Land asset management and storage management are service projects, not separate project types and not reduced to thread-like surfaces.

### Threads, Standalone Events

Not all activity needs a full project.

The platform may also support:

- Threads for open discussion, coordination, and tagged local conversation
- Standalone events for gatherings, classes, celebrations, protests, meetups, or other social coordination that does not need full project governance

These still feed discovery, moderation, and demand signals, but they should not be mistaken for full governed projects by default.

Standalone events are distinct from project activity. Project activity happens inside a project and supports its ongoing work through scheduled sessions, meetings, review points, or other coordination. Standalone events remain lightweight social or organizational surfaces and should not be treated as projects by default.

Ongoing service work, even when modest in scope, should still be organized as a project rather than reduced to a thread or a standalone event.

### Awareness Signals Versus Demand Signals

The platform will keep lightweight awareness or visibility signals on content such as threads, standalone events, and projects.

These signals mean a user wants to boost visibility, mark something as useful, or support awareness of the content itself.

They are not the same as project demand signals.

For projects in the researching stage, the interface should expose a separate demand-signal action that records that a user supports, needs, or is likely to participate in the actual project output or work itself.

If both appear on the same project surface, they must remain visually and semantically separate.

Neither awareness signals nor demand signals should be mistaken for a formal governance vote.

### Roles

Manager responsibilities:

- Edit the project
- Post updates
- Manage project activity
- Make proposals
- Vote on proposals
- Create or edit collective fund drives
- Propose role changes

Member responsibilities:

- Make proposals
- Vote on proposals

Project activity means internal scheduled coordination inside a project, such as work sessions, check-ins, planning meetings, review meetings, or other work-linked gatherings. It is not the same thing as a standalone event.

Any user can join a project and become a member. Role changes, including promoting a member to manager or removing a manager, require a passed vote. No project role is permanent.

Project managers are accountable through ordinary project governance and may also be subject to a visible confidence signal on the project itself.

Project managers may step down at any time. Members may nominate themselves as replacements through the platform's normal governance process. If no replacement is confirmed immediately, the project may remain temporarily without a manager until governance resolves it.

## Communities

Communities are optional social and organizational spaces for groups with a shared place, identity, or affiliation.

They are not asset-holding units, do not control production as a bloc, and do not vote as a separate body over the rest of the platform.

Current planning defaults:

- Communities are open by default, but may be closed
- Closed communities may use membership votes for admission
- Communities follow the same platform-wide governance rules rather than a separate constitution
- Communities may connect themselves to projects for discovery and discussion, but individual users still vote and signal as ordinary members
- Community-only discussion spaces may exist without giving the community separate ownership rights

## Voting Model

Every platform decision uses the same broad voting mechanism, whether the decision concerns project governance, distribution plans, moderation, role changes, or wider platform matters.

### Ratio-Based Voting

Members can vote yes, no, or abstain on all competing proposals independently. The outcome is determined by the broadest genuine support rather than a simple highest raw total.

A proposal must:

1. Meet the required participation threshold.
2. Be ranked by approval ratio, calculated as yes votes divided by total votes cast on that proposal.

If multiple proposals clear the threshold, the highest approval ratio wins.

### Discussion During Voting

Discussion does not close when voting opens.

Members may continue discussing, commenting, and even submitting new proposals while a vote is active unless a later rule for a specific decision type explicitly narrows that behavior.

### Participation Thresholds

Current planning thresholds for project decisions are:

- Fewer than 10 members: 100% participation
- 10 to 50 members: 60% participation
- 51 to 100 members: 40% participation
- 101 to 999 members: 25% participation
- 1000 or more members: 10% participation

High-stakes decisions may also require an absolute minimum vote count in addition to the percentage threshold.

The current planning default is that a proposal creator or project manager may set such a minimum when opening a vote, but the exact rule and guardrails are still draft-sensitive.

### Deadlock and Failure

If no proposal clears the threshold, the voting period extends automatically. If the threshold is still not met, the matter returns to discussion before resubmission.

## Proposal Types

The following decisions require a passed proposal vote:

- Changes to project rules or structure
- Distribution plan selection
- Promoting a member to manager
- Removing a manager
- Removing a member from a project
- Creating or modifying a fund drive
- Moderation actions above routine housekeeping
- Director appointments and removals at the foundation level

## Moderation and Organizational Scope

Posts and projects are tagged to channels representing productive spheres, topics, or locations. This tagging system helps discovery, self-organization, and moderation.

Moderation is designed as a delegated function, not an authority class. Moderator roles are revocable and accountable to the community.

The current planning default is channel-based moderation. Threads, standalone events, projects, and communities route reports through the channels they are tagged to.

### Moderator Role And Standing

- Each channel should have moderators where volunteers exist, but a channel may remain temporarily unmoderated if nobody volunteers
- Users may nominate themselves for moderator roles and may step down at any time
- Moderators are expected to remove spam, review reports, and apply narrow channel-specific restrictions before broader sanctions are considered
- Moderator standing should be visible on the channel page as an ongoing support ratio
- The planning default is that moderators remain in role only while above a positive-support threshold, likely around 70 percent, but the exact threshold and dismissal timing remain draft-sensitive
- Moderator roles may be visible on profiles, but individual support or opposition votes should remain private

### Moderation Principles

- Full exclusion is a last resort because platform access is tied to participation in production
- Harm reduction and accountability are preferred over social ostracism
- Moderation actions are reviewable and recorded on-chain where appropriate
- Voting privacy is preserved for ordinary users, while moderator standing and support should remain visible enough for accountability

### Reports And Accountability

- Reports about users and project managers route to moderators of the channel or channels the relevant content or project is tagged to
- Reports about a channel moderator should route to the other moderators of that channel where possible, while remaining reviewable through broader governance
- Project managers remain removable or replaceable through passed governance decisions when confidence fails

### Escalation Path

Preferred responses to harmful behavior, in order of severity:

1. Community reporting and channel-moderator review
2. Temporary channel-specific silences or posting restrictions for a defined period
3. Removal from specific projects by member vote
4. Platform-wide restrictions through governance
5. Platform-wide ban for the most serious and sustained harmful behavior

Narrow restrictions are preferred over full exclusion. A user who is chat-restricted should not lose voting or meeting access by default when a narrower response is enough.

The broad moderation scope is now channel-based, but exact confidence thresholds, automatic-dismissal behavior, and appeal mechanics remain draft-sensitive.

## Production Priorities

The platform supports production across all domains, but some spheres are strategically foundational because they reduce dependence on capitalist systems most directly.

Priority spheres include:

- Food
- Housing
- Healthcare
- Communications
- Innovation

These areas are not exclusive. They are the most important early focus because they create the material conditions for broader participation.

## Project Lifecycle

Every project follows a visible lifecycle.

### Researching

The project is proposed and the community gauges interest. This stage is where the most important early demand signal appears on the project itself. Users can signal interest with a simple action on the project, indicating support, need, or likely involvement. Similar existing projects should be surfaced to encourage collaboration instead of duplicate efforts.

Researching is the default starting stage for a newly created project.

The product should avoid using the word `proposal` as the main user-facing stage label for a project in this state, because proposals are already separate formal governance objects elsewhere in the platform.

The user-facing action in this stage should make clear that demand signalling is not the same as lightweight awareness and not the same as a formal governance proposal vote.

### Planning

Productive plans and distributive plans are submitted, discussed, revised, and voted on. Only after agreed plans are finalized does the project move to the next stage.

### Funding

If materials or equipment are required, a fund drive is opened. Contributions are held in escrow by the foundation until the goal is met. If the goal fails, contributions are returned.

### Producing

Active production is underway. Tasks are tracked collectively so members can see what is happening, what is blocked, and where help is needed.

### Ongoing

Some projects, such as tool libraries, storage facilities, or long-lived resource efforts, may remain ongoing instead of moving quickly to a final completed state.

An ongoing project stays active until it is replaced, dissolved, or explicitly closed through governance.

### Completed

Production is finished and output is ready for allocation.

### Distributing

The approved distribution plan is executed and tracked.

### Distributed

The distribution cycle is complete. The full record remains available as planning input for future projects.

### Updates And Checkpoints

Managers should post visible updates as projects cross meaningful checkpoints such as fund-drive progress, stage changes, and important production milestones.

These updates should refresh the project timeline and help feed and notification surfaces reflect real movement instead of only the original creation date.

## Task Tracking

Active projects use structured task tracking similar to a project board, where work is broken into tasks and statuses such as to do, in progress, and done.

This serves two purposes:

- Operational visibility for the project itself
- Aggregate planning data for future projects

Individual labor is not converted into entitlement. Labor tracking exists for planning, not for exchange or credit.

## Distribution Model

All outputs are distributed directly to community members based on need and agreed plans. Labor contribution does not determine distribution.

### Distribution Types

Two models are available when planning output distribution:

- Library: borrow and return for durable goods such as tools, equipment, and shared spaces
- Distribution: receive a share for consumables such as food, materials, and supplies

A project may use both models for different outputs.

### Plan Submission and Adoption

1. A project is created and members express interest through signals.
2. A detailed plan is submitted covering location, required materials, and expected produce.
3. Members submit proposals covering quantities, recipient criteria, distribution type, and scaling procedures.
4. Members vote using the platform-wide ratio voting system.
5. The winning plan is adopted and recorded.

### Scaling Rules

Every finite or variable output plan should define what happens in shortfall and surplus conditions before production begins.

Shortfall options can include:

- Proportional reduction across recipients
- Using a pre-agreed emergency response procedure for urgent cases
- Using buffer stock built from prior surpluses

Surplus options can include:

- Community buffers
- Proportional redistribution
- A new distribution round

These rules should be agreed in advance and recorded so there is no ambiguity during execution.

### Indivisible Goods

Some goods cannot be reduced proportionally. Housing is the clearest example.

The current model is:

- Access handled through a visible waitlist
- Waitlist length used as a planning signal for future projects
- Project plans define in advance how genuine emergency situations are handled without creating a standing priority-needs distribution system for ordinary allocation
- Allocations reviewed periodically through governance rather than unilateral decision-making

This is not a permanent priority-needs model for distribution. Emergency handling should be built into the project plan itself as a clear, limited procedure for exceptional situations.

### Queue And Readiness Rules

- The platform should not use a standing priority-needs verification panel
- Ordinary distribution should follow clear plan-defined queue rules built from visible signals such as waitlists, confirmed interest, or other adopted criteria
- For storage or warehouse requests where stock is scarce, FIFO is the current planning default unless a passed plan sets another rule
- Recipients should confirm readiness close to dispatch
- If a recipient does not respond within the defined period, the allocation may pass to the next eligible recipient or return to collective stock according to the plan

### Delivery

Distribution must support:

- Physical goods delivered to a registered address kept private from public views
- Digital goods delivered through download or email
- Library items with return flows back into collective stock

Distribution records should preserve a status history and recipient-readiness state instead of overwriting prior states.

## Demand Signals and Planning

Production is informed by visible need rather than price signals. The most immediate signal is direct interest shown on a project while it is still in the researching stage.

This demand signal should remain separate from any lightweight awareness or visibility signal on the same project surface.

Formal governance still happens through proposals and votes, not through demand-signal totals alone.

Key planning inputs include:

- Project interest signals during the researching stage
- Interest shown through tagged threads when those clearly express demand or coordination need
- Project suggestion lists
- Waitlists
- Inter-project links and downstream recipient requests
- Community-linked project interest where relevant
- Failed project signals
- Distribution history
- Production history and aggregate effort data

These signals help the community identify what should be proposed, funded, and built next.

## Inter-Project Resource Flows And Storage

Collective resources may move between projects without becoming private exchanges. These movements should stay visible as part of production planning and execution.

### Custody, Location, And Project Responsibility

Assets, produce, tools, equipment, facilities, and other means of production remain collectively owned even when they are physically attached to, stored on, or otherwise tied to a land asset.

Land identifies the physical place where something is situated, stored, used, or rests in the world. It is not a separate basis of ownership over the resources tied to that site.

The word steward in this document should refer to the non-profit stewardship layer and its board members, not to ordinary project managers.

Members of asset management service projects handle custody, storage, maintenance, scheduling, access, and handoff inside normal platform operations. They do not become owners of the underlying resources by performing those functions.

### Resource Transfers

- A project member initiates a transfer or access request
- The request routes to the relevant asset management service project, or another relevant service project, responsible for custody, access, scheduling, or dispatch
- Members of that service project confirm the request and later confirm receipt, pickup, access, or dispatch as appropriate
- If a transfer already fits the approved production or distribution plan, a separate vote is not required for each movement
- The authorization and execution record should stay on-chain, while the local database stores operational detail

### Asset Management Service Projects

Warehouses, libraries, land-custody projects, and similar collective inventory hubs should be organized as asset management service projects rather than as a separate project type.

They may receive deposits from production projects, hold stock on behalf of the collective, coordinate land access, and dispatch materials or goods to other projects or distribution flows.

They are still projects like everything else on the platform, with members, updates, proposals, and governance.

Their role is custodial and operational. They manage storage conditions, access, queueing, dispatch, and land- or asset-handoff rules, but they do not acquire ownership over the stock they hold.

Current planning defaults:

- Stored stock remains collectively owned and never becomes a separate treasury
- Limited stock should follow clear queue rules, with FIFO as the current default for storage requests unless governance defines another rule
- Inventory review should account for expiry, hazard, and storage conditions where relevant

## Public And Private Data Expectations

- Public by default: project details, public posts and threads, distribution allocations, moderation actions, funding totals, and visible standing for managers or moderators where shown
- Private by default: direct messages, delivery addresses, detailed personal contact data, and individual contribution amounts unless a contributor chooses public acknowledgement
- Voting participation may be visible where needed for governance, but individual vote choices should not be displayed on user profiles
- Users should control whether name, location precision, and contact details are broadly visible
- Account deletion should preserve public participation history in anonymized form rather than erasing the collective record

## Failed or Dissolved Projects

If a project fails before completion, funds held in escrow by the foundation are returned to contributors. The failure remains visible in the record and unmet need feeds back into future planning.

## Collective Ownership

Everything produced through the platform belongs to the community. Materials acquired through collective funding, outputs produced through collective labor, and infrastructure built through collective effort are held in trust by the foundation on the community's behalf.

This remains true even when produce, equipment, tools, storage, workshops, or other means of production are physically tied to a land site or managed by an asset management service project. Land records location. The relevant service project records custody and access responsibility. Ownership remains collective.

No individual acquires private ownership over collectively produced outputs by default.

## Transition Model

The platform does not require an immediate break from existing systems. It is designed to grow alongside capitalism at first, gradually reducing dependence on it as productive capacity grows.

Early participation may look like supplementary production such as food distribution, tool libraries, housing projects, or community services. Over time, more needs can be met outside the market, making capitalist participation less necessary for survival.

The goal is to make participation in capitalism a choice rather than a necessity.
